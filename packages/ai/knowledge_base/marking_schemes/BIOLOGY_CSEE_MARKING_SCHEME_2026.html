(a) Three structural differences between Plant Cell and Animal Cell: [3 Marks]

Award 1 mark for each correctly paired structural difference ($3 \times 1 = 3\text{ marks}$):

Plant Cell Animal Cell
Has a rigid cellulose cell wall outside the cell membrane. Lacks a cell wall; bounded only by a cell membrane.

(b) Primary functions of cell structures: [6 Marks]
(i) Cell wall: Provides mechanical support, structural rigidity, protection to the inner contents, and determines cell shape. [2 Marks]

QUESTION 4 [Total: 09 Marks]
(a) Definition and Importance of Balanced Diet: [3 Marks]
• Definition: A balanced diet is a meal containing all essential nutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, vitamins, minerals, water, and roughage) in their correct biological proportions. [1 Mark]
• Importance (Award 1 mark each for any two points): [2 Marks]
  1. Provides energy needed for metabolic processes and physical activities.
  2. Boosts immune function to protect the body against deficiency diseases and infections.
(b) Digestion of cooked cassava (starch): [6 Marks]
• Mouth: Cassava is masticated physically by teeth. Salivary glands secrete salivary amylase (ptyalin) which digests cooked starch into maltose under alkaline/neutral pH. [2 Marks]
• Stomach: Amylase action stops due to acidic Hydrochloric acid ($\text{HCl}$). No digestion of starch occurs in the stomach; food turns into semi-fluid chyme. [1 Mark]
• Duodenum: Chyme is neutralized by bile salts and pancreatic juice. Pancreatic amylase hydrolyzes remaining undigested starch into maltose. [2 Marks]
• Ileum (End product): Intestinal enzyme maltase hydrolyzes maltose into simple absorbable glucose. [1 Mark]
QUESTION 5 [Total: 09 Marks]
(i) Thicker muscular wall of Left Ventricle: [3 Marks]
The left ventricle pumps blood through the systemic circulation to all distant body tissues/organs (high resistance circuit), which requires generating high hydrostatic pressure. In contrast, the right ventricle pumps blood over a shorter distance to the lungs (pulmonary circuit) under low pressure. [3 Marks]
(ii) Pathway of a Red Blood Cell: [4 Marks]
Deoxygenated blood from Vena Cava $\rightarrow$ enters Right Atrium $\rightarrow$ passes through Tricuspid Valve $\rightarrow$ flows into Right Ventricle $\rightarrow$ pumped past Semilunar Valve into Pulmonary Artery towards lungs. [Award 1 mark for each correct sequence step up to 4 marks]
(iii) Role of Tricuspid Valve: [2 Marks]
Closes during right ventricular contraction (systole) to prevent the backflow of deoxygenated blood into the right atrium. [2 Marks]
QUESTION 6 [Total: 09 Marks]
(a) Definition and Distinction: [3 Marks]
• Gaseous Exchange: Physical process of exchanging oxygen and carbon dioxide across a respiratory surface by diffusion. [1.5 Marks]
• Cellular Respiration: Chemical breakdown of respiratory substrates (like glucose) inside living cells to release energy (ATP). [1.5 Marks]
(b) Three Structural Adaptations of Alveoli: [6 Marks]
Award 2 marks for each well-explained adaptation (1 mark for naming, 1 mark for explanation):
1. Thin alveolar wall (one-cell thick): Reduces the diffusion distance for rapid gas movement. [2 Marks]
2. Dense capillary network: Maintains a steep concentration gradient between alveolar air and blood. [2 Marks]
3. Moist epithelial lining: Dissolves oxygen before it diffuses across the respiratory membrane. [2 Marks]

QUESTION 9 [Total: 15 Marks]
(a) Mendel's First Law (Law of Segregation): [3 Marks]
States that characteristics of an organism are determined by internal factors (alleles) occurring in pairs, and during gamete formation, these paired alleles segregate so that each gamete carries only one allele of the pair. [3 Marks]
(b) Monohybrid Cross (Heterozygous Tall $Tt \times$ Dwarf $tt$): [8 Marks]
• Parental Phenotypes: Tall pea plant  $\times$  Dwarf pea plant
• Parental Genotypes: $Tt$  $\times$  $tt$ [1.5 Marks]
• Gametes: $(T), (t)$  $\times$  $(t), (t)$ [1.5 Marks]
• Punnett Square Layout: [3 Marks]
Gametes $T$ $t$
$t$ $Tt$ (Tall) $tt$ (Dwarf)
$t$ $Tt$ (Tall) $tt$ (Dwarf)
• Genotypic Ratio: $1\, Tt : 1\, tt$ (50% Heterozygous Tall : 50% Homozygous Dwarf). [1 Mark]
• Phenotypic Ratio: $1\text{ Tall} : 1\text{ Dwarf}$ (1:1 Ratio). [1 Mark]
(c) Sex-linked Genes and Disorders: [4 Marks]
• Definition: Genes carried exclusively on sex chromosomes (usually X-chromosome) without homologous counterparts on the Y-chromosome. [2 Marks]
• Two Examples: Haemophilia, Red-green color blindness. [2 Marks]

QUESTION 10 [Total: 15 Marks]
(i) Ultrafiltration at Renal Corpuscle: [5 Marks]
High hydrostatic pressure is created in the glomerulus because the afferent arteriole lumen is wider than the efferent arteriole lumen. This pressure forces water, glucose, amino acids, mineral salts, and urea out of glomerular capillaries through basement membrane pores into Bowman's capsule space, forming glomerular filtrate. Large blood cells and plasma proteins are retained. [5 Marks]
(ii) Selective Reabsorption in PCT: [5 Marks]
All glucose, amino acids, key ions, and water are reabsorbed back into peritubular capillaries from PCT. Glucose and amino acids are reabsorbed by active transport against concentration gradient (utilizing ATP and microvilli surface area). Water is reabsorbed passively by osmosis. [5 Marks]
(iii) Role of ADH in Osmoregulation: [5 Marks]
When body fluids have low water potential (dehydration), osmoreceptors in hypothalamus stimulate posterior pituitary gland to secrete ADH into blood. ADH increases permeability of distal convoluted tubules and collecting ducts to water. More water is reabsorbed into blood, resulting in concentrated, low-volume urine. [5 Marks]
QUESTION 11 [Total: 15 Marks]
(a) Lamarck's Theory and Criticisms: [6 Marks]
• Theory: Organs used frequently become more developed and stronger, while disused organs degenerate. Acquired traits are inherited by offspring. [3 Marks]
• Two Criticisms: Acquired somatic modifications (e.g., muscular body, cut tail) do not alter germ cells/DNA and cannot be inherited. Phenotypic changes caused by environment are non-heritable. [3 Marks]
(b) Darwin's Theory of Natural Selection (5 Steps): [6 Marks]
1. Overproduction of offspring: Organisms produce more offspring than can survive. [1 Mark]
2. Struggle for existence: Limited resources lead to competition. [1 Mark]
3. Variation: Individuals exhibit structural/functional variations. [1 Mark]
4. Survival of the fittest: Favorable variations confer survival advantages. [1.5 Marks]
5. Inheritance & Speciation: Survivors reproduce and pass favorable traits to offspring. [1.5 Marks]
(c) Three Anatomical Evidences of Evolution: [3 Marks]
1. Homologous structures (e.g., pentadactyl limb structure in vertebrates). [1 Mark]
2. Analogous structures (e.g., wings of birds and insects). [1 Mark]
3. Vestigial organs (e.g., human appendix, nictitating membrane). [1 Mark]
