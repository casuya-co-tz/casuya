Introduction Writes fractions on the board: ½, ¾, ⅔, ⁵⁄₃, ⁷⁄₄, 2½, 3¾.

Asks: "What do you notice about these fractions? Are they all the same?"

Draws a pizza diagram showing ¾ and ⁵⁄₃ to illustrate different fraction types. 		Observe the fractions written on the board.

Attempt to describe the differences they notice.

Respond to the teacher's questions. 		5 Fractions are correctly classified as proper, improper, or mixed numbers
Competence Development Explains that a proper fraction has a numerator smaller than the denominator (e.g., ½, ⅔, ¾).

Explains that an improper fraction has a numerator greater than or equal to the denominator (e.g., ⁵⁄₃, ⁷⁄₄, ⁹⁄₉).

Explains that a mixed number consists of a whole number and a proper fraction (e.g., 1½, 2¾).

Demonstrates conversion: ⁵⁄₃ = 1²⁄₃ using diagrams. 		Listen and take notes on the definitions.

Observe fraction circles and bars to understand each type.

Practice identifying proper, improper, and mixed numbers from examples.

Attempt converting improper fractions to mixed numbers. 		15 Fractions are correctly classified as proper, improper, or mixed numbers
Design Organises students into groups of five.

Provides each group with fraction cards to sort into three categories: proper, improper, and mixed numbers.

Monitors group work and guides students who need help. 		Work collaboratively in groups to sort fraction cards.

Discuss and agree on the classification of each fraction.

Present their sorted categories to the class. 		10 Fractions are correctly classified as proper, improper, or mixed numbers
Realisation Uses oral questions and answers to assess understanding.

Gives a short quiz with 5 fractions to classify.

Summarises the lesson by reviewing the three types of fractions. 		Respond to oral questions.

Complete the classification quiz.

Ask questions where unclear. 		10 Fractions are correctly classified as proper, improper, or mixed numbers
